1. Executive Summary

Shilfa (شِلفة — Egyptian slang for a fresh haircut) is a mobile-first barber booking and relationship app built for the Egyptian men’s grooming market. Unlike marketplace-first apps (Glamera, Fresha) that focus on discovering new providers, Shilfa is built around the core insight that Egyptian men are deeply loyal to their barber — they don’t need to FIND a barber, they need to BOOK the one they already have without wasting 1–2 hours waiting.

The app serves three user types: Clients who book and manage their barber relationship, Barbers who manage their schedule and showcase their work, and Shop Owners who oversee their team. The QR check-in system, style memory with photos, and dual-profile portfolio are the core differentiators that no existing app delivers — especially not in Arabic.



1.1 Why This Works in Egypt

Men’s haircut prices have surged: 150–300 EGP at local barbers, 300–500+ EGP at premium shops — making commission/subscription economics viable

Every popular barber in Egypt has the same problem: 5–8 guys waiting on the couch, some leave, no scheduling system exists

Barbers already use Instagram as a portfolio — Shilfa gives them a functional portfolio that’s tied to real bookings and real clients

Zero competition: theCut (US), SQUIRE (US), Barberly (US) don’t operate in MENA. Glamera targets women. Fresha is generic and not localized.

Egyptian men visit barbers every 2–4 weeks — that’s 12–26 transactions/year per user, comparable to food delivery frequency

4. QR Check-In System — Core Differentiator

The QR system is the heart of Shilfa. It replaces the awkward "I’m here" phone call and creates a verifiable, time-stamped check-in that benefits both parties.



4.1 Flow

Client books 4:00 PM slot with barber Ahmed

At 3:45 PM, client’s app generates a dynamic QR code with a countdown timer

Client arrives, opens app, shows QR to barber Ahmed

Ahmed opens his barber app, taps "Scan", scans the QR

System verifies: correct client, correct barber, within time window → check-in confirmed

Ahmed’s screen shows Aly’s client card: preferred style, last cut photo, notes

If QR is not scanned by 4:15 PM, it expires and the slot is released back to availability



4.2 Technical Design

QR encodes a signed JWT token containing: booking_id, client_id, barber_id, valid_from, valid_until

Token is signed server-side so it can’t be forged or reused

QR regenerates every 30 seconds with a new token (prevents screenshot sharing)

Barber’s scanner validates the token locally against cached booking data (works offline briefly)

On successful scan: booking status updates to “arrived”, client gets confirmation push notification



4.3 No-Show Handling

If QR expires without scan: booking auto-cancels, slot reopens, client gets “missed appointment” notification

Repeat no-shows (3+ in 30 days) trigger a soft flag visible to barbers (“this client has missed recent appointments”)

Barbers can optionally require a small deposit (via Fawry/Paymob) for flagged clients — P2 feature



5. Dual Portfolio System

This is Shilfa’s social layer — the feature that makes it sticky and shareable.



5.1 How It Works

After a cut, the barber takes a photo using the Shilfa app camera

The photo is tagged with: client name, service performed, date, and optionally the style name

The photo appears on BOTH the barber’s portfolio AND the client’s style history

The barber’s portfolio becomes a living gallery of real work with real clients (not just Instagram reposts)

The client’s profile becomes a visual timeline of their haircut evolution

Next visit: client opens app, taps “Show last cut”, barber sees exactly what was done — no guessing



5.2 Why This Matters

For barbers: their portfolio grows automatically with every client. Better than Instagram because it’s verified work.

For clients: “I want the same thing as last time” is now a tap, not a 5-minute explanation

For Shilfa: every photo is organic content that drives app virality when shared on social media

Shilfa monetizes from the barber/shop side, never from clients. The app is completely free for customers.

9.2 Why Barbers Will Pay

299 EGP/month = the revenue from 2 haircuts. If Shilfa prevents just 3 walk-away customers per month (who left because of long wait), it pays for itself 2x over.

The portfolio feature replaces the need for a separate Instagram business strategy — barbers get a professional portfolio that drives real bookings

Style memory reduces miscommunication and callbacks — happier clients = more tips + more loyalty



10. Go-To-Market Strategy



10.1 Phase 1: Seed (Month 1–2)

Personally onboard 5–10 popular barbers in New Cairo / Nasr City / Heliopolis who have the "always full, long wait" problem

Offer free Pro for 3 months in exchange for feedback + letting you observe their workflow

Each barber tells their regulars to download the app to book — organic client-side growth from barber network

Target: 5–10 barbers, 100–300 client downloads



10.2 Phase 2: Traction (Month 3–6)

TikTok / Instagram Reels content: "POV: You booked your barber on Shilfa and walked straight to the chair" — this is highly shareable content

Partner with barber influencers on TikTok (Egyptian barber TikTok is HUGE — millions of views)

Every post-cut photo uploaded through Shilfa is watermarked with subtle branding — free organic marketing

Target: 50–100 barbers, 2,000–5,000 client downloads



10.3 Phase 3: Scale (Month 6–12)

Expand to Alexandria, Mansoura, Tanta — university cities with young male demographics

Launch "Shilfa Verified Barber" badge for quality-vetted barbers — trust signal

Introduce women’s salon vertical if demand validates (same tech, different branding)

Explore GCC expansion (Saudi, UAE) where men’s grooming spend is 5–10x Egypt



11. Key Risks & Mitigations

Risk

Severity

Mitigation

Barbers resist adopting an app

HIGH

In-person onboarding. Show the ROI: "you lost 3 clients today who left because of the wait." Free tier removes cost barrier.

Clients won’t download another app

MEDIUM

The barber is the distribution channel. When YOUR barber says "download this to book me," you download it. Barber-driven adoption.

QR system feels over-engineered

LOW

Make it dead simple: open app → show QR → done in 3 seconds. Fallback: barber can manually confirm arrival.

Glamera/Fresha copies the men’s vertical

LOW

Glamera has been women-focused for 6 years. Pivoting brand identity is extremely hard. First-mover advantage in men’s niche.

Egyptian men won’t pre-book (walk-in culture)

HIGH

The target isn’t ALL men. It’s the ones already frustrated by waiting 1–2 hours. Start with the pain, not the habit.

Low revenue per user

MEDIUM

Monetize the barber, not the client. 299 EGP/month from 500 barbers = 149,500 EGP MRR. Viable.

13. Post-MVP Roadmap

Loyalty program: "Your 10th cut is free" — barber-configured, tracked automatically through bookings

Walk-in live queue: Display board (tablet at shop entrance) showing current wait time and queue position

In-app tipping: Digital tips via Fawry/Paymob after the cut is completed

AI style suggestions: Upload a selfie → AI suggests hairstyles based on face shape — highly viral feature

Barber-to-barber social: Barbers follow each other, share techniques, build professional community

Women’s vertical: Same core tech, different branding (“Shilfa Beauty” or separate app)

GCC expansion: Saudi Arabia and UAE where men’s grooming is a $2B+ market with higher ARPU

Product sales: Barbers sell hair products through the app (pomade, wax, beard oil) — e-commerce layer
